01 The memoir

New memoir

A Richmond story with universal reach

The distance between surviving and truly living.

In From Surviving to Living: Globetown to Greatness, Rodney “Alamo” Brown traces a life shaped by hardship—and remade through faith, sobriety, service, and purpose.

Rooted in Richmond’s Townhouses, known as Globetown, the memoir follows Brown through tragedy, addiction, depression, and personal transformation. It is an honest account of rising beyond circumstance without forgetting the community that made the journey possible.

Grace in the Storm.

One of the darkest moments of my life brought me back to the very place where my story began.

I was hospitalized at Herrick Hospital in Berkeley for alcohol poisoning. The irony wasn’t lost on me. It was the same hospital where I was born, and years later, the same hospital where I stood beside my father and made the heartbreaking decision to remove life support after his battle with cocaine addiction.

As I lay in that hospital bed, I was overwhelmed with emotion. I was conflicted, scared, and forced to confront the reality of my own mortality. I knew something had to change.

Continue reading Chapter 9

The doctors told me I was fortunate. Despite years of alcohol abuse, there was no significant damage to my liver. They gave me a warning that I will never forget: “You should never drink alcohol again.”

For three days, I remained in that hospital reflecting on my life, my choices, and my future. During those moments of uncertainty, I felt the presence of God in a way I had never experienced before. I truly believe God saved me from death, damnation, and destruction.

Before I left that hospital, I made a covenant with God. I promised Him that I would never take another drink of alcohol for the rest of my life. That promise was made 26 years ago.

Through triumphs and trials, celebrations and disappointments, I have kept that promise. Not because I am perfect, but because God’s grace has been sufficient. What could have been the end of my story became the beginning of a new chapter—one marked by purpose, service, faith, and redemption.

Looking back now, I understand that the hospital room I feared would become my final destination was actually the place where God gave me a second chance at life.

02 The journey

Globetown to greatness

One life.
Many chapters.

  1. 01

    Richmond roots

    Raised in the Townhouses neighborhood—Globetown—Brown learned early how hardship can either narrow a life or deepen its purpose.

  2. 02

    A working life in service

    Education, human resources, mental health care, barbering, and community outreach became different ways to meet people where they were.

  3. 03

    Sports, music & storytelling

    His path included managing Sons of Funk, writing for 247Sports and CBS Sports Digital Media, and scouting for the Atlanta Braves and Tampa Bay Devil Rays.

  4. 04

    Community at scale

    Brown organized nine churches to distribute approximately 2.5 million meals across West Contra Costa County.

  5. 05

    A tradition of unity

    He founded Soulful Softball Sunday, bringing thousands to Nicholl Park for sports, education, health resources, youth activities, and celebration.

  6. 06

    The story continues

    Recognition followed, but service remained the mission. His new memoir carries that message from Richmond to readers everywhere.
